# On Violence, Regret, and Preparation

## A Personal Reflection

There are actions that change who a person is in the eyes of others. For me, **someone who kills another person is no longer a person** in the moral sense. The act itself strips away that status.

## Sorry Is Not Enough

- 😔 **Feeling sorry afterwards does not undo what was done.**
- ⏳ **Regret comes too late** — after a life has already been taken.
- ⚖️ **Justice and remorse are not the same thing.** One can feel guilt and still owe a debt that can never be repaid.

## Think Before You Act

The real responsibility is **before** the moment of violence:

- 🧠 **Think before you act.**
- 🧠 **Think before you speak.**
- 🧠 **Think before you escalate.**

Most tragedies are not born from pure evil — they are born from moments where someone stopped thinking.

## Prepare in Your Head

One way to prevent violence is to **prepare situations in your mind before they happen**:

- What will you do if someone provokes you?
- What will you do if you feel rage rising?
- What will you do if you have a weapon within reach?
- What will you do if alcohol, pride, or fear clouds your judgment?

When you have already decided — in calm moments — that you will not kill, that you will walk away, that you will call for help, then the dangerous moment loses much of its power over you.

## The Line That Cannot Be Crossed

> *There are lines that, once crossed, cannot be uncrossed. Killing another human being is one of them.*

No apology, no explanation, no time in prison fully restores what was broken. That is why the only real protection is prevention.

## What This Means

| Reactive Person | Prepared Person |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| Acts on impulse | Has already decided what they will and won't do |
| Says "I was angry" | Says "I trained myself not to act on anger" |
| Regrets after | Prevents before |
| Depends on the moment | Depends on inner discipline |

## Key Insight

**Sorry is not a safety net. Preparation is.** The time to decide who you are is not in the middle of a crisis — it is every ordinary day before it.
