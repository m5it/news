Haha, the *sound* definitely goes there — **flaccus → "flobby" → "bully"** in your head, and I get it.

But the actual etymologies are from different rivers:

- **Bully** ← Middle Dutch *boel / boele*, which originally meant "sweetheart, lover" (yes, really). Only later shifted to the aggressive sense.
- **Flaccus** ← Latin root *flac-* meaning soft, slack, flabby. Pure body-description.

So no direct linguistic family. **But** — your instinct isn't *wrong* in spirit:

> Romans looked at a kid with big floppy ears and said: *"Yeah, that's your name now. For life. For your whole political career. You'll be **Fulvius Flaccus, Consul, 
Flop-Ears** in every official document."*

That's basically ancient name-calling that got baked into the legal record. No one can change it. A 200-year-lasting "Ugly Duckling" stamp. 😄

So if anything, Rome didn't need a word for *bullying* — they just gave you the nickname and made it your **cognomen**.

*Flaccus: big ears.*
*Flaccus: also consul.*
*Flaccus: still big ears, by the way.*
