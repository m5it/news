# Ceuta Immigrants: A Terrifying Situation

## The Observation

The view on immigrants in **Ceuta** is terrifying.

## What Is Happening

People are stranded in desperate conditions. If no one starts helping soon, the situation could turn from a humanitarian crisis into something even worse.

> **If nobody helps them, they will start picking up bodies from the beaches.**

## A Hard Question

When you see suffering like this, a question rises up:

> **Where is Allah? Where is Jesus? Where is God when this happens?**

For people who believe, this is one of the hardest tests of faith. You pray, you trust, you hope — and then you watch children, women, and men die at a border.

Maybe the answer is not that the divine is absent. Maybe the answer is that **we are the hands that were supposed to help**. Maybe the test is not for God to act, but for us to stop waiting and start acting.

## Why This Matters

- 🌍 **Human lives are at risk** — not statistics, not politics, but people.
- ⏰ **Time is running out** — without aid, death becomes inevitable.
- 🤝 **Inaction is a choice** — and it is the wrong one.
- 🙏 **Faith without action is empty** — every religion teaches care for the stranger and the suffering.

## The Bigger Picture

This is not just about Ceuta. It is about how Europe, Morocco, and the world respond when vulnerable people arrive at the border. The current response looks like waiting, blocking, and hoping the problem goes away.

But people do not just disappear. And the sea does not forgive.

## What Should Happen

| Instead of... | There should be... |
|---------------|---------------------|
| Closed borders | Safe passage and asylum processing |
| Waiting | Emergency aid, water, food, medical care |
| Blame | Cooperation between Spain, Morocco, and EU |
| Silence | International attention and pressure |

## Key Insight

**A border policy that lets people die is not a policy — it is a death sentence.** Helping now is cheaper in every way than picking up the consequences later.

If there is a God, perhaps He is watching to see whether we will be merciful. If there is no God, then mercy is entirely up to us — which makes it even more urgent.
